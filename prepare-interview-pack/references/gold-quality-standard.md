# Gold Quality Standard

Use this as the acceptance standard for a finished interview handbook.

## Ready to Speak

- Write the 60- and 90-second introductions as complete natural scripts.
- Write two complete 2–3 minute project stories.
- Write at least ten to twelve complete high-probability answers.
- Place memory bullets after scripts, never instead of scripts.
- Add a transition into each project, a stop sentence, and optional expansion directions.

## Role Translation

- Lead with the candidate story most directly connected to the target business problem.
- Translate every project into the target role's language without changing its facts.
- Do not choose a polished process or system story over a less polished but more direct operating story.
- If the direct proof is missing, label the best story provisional and request the missing case.

## Persuasion and Safety

- Establish why the candidate fits before discussing gaps.
- Keep evidence and strengths dominant; keep warnings compact.
- Pair every gap with adjacent proof, learning method, and early action.
- Preserve ownership, metric, period, denominator, and attribution boundaries.

## Interview Usability

- Put an independently usable Battle Card before the full handbook.
- Keep the complete introduction and story memory cards adjacent; do not interrupt them with company analysis.
- Separate P0, P1, and P2 by rehearsal priority, not by whether content exists.
- Include likely follow-ups and factual anchors for each core story.
- Include motivation, role understanding, project depth, business judgment, collaboration, leadership, failure or conflict, gap, departure, and career-break questions when relevant.
- End with a short rehearsal schedule and final claim card.
- Include a compact company-and-role STARS map even when the interview is imminent.

## Failure Conditions

Reject and revise the handbook when any condition is true:

- Core questions contain only bullet outlines.
- Fewer than ten high-probability answers are provided without a user request for brevity.
- The primary story does not address the target role's central problem and is not labeled provisional.
- Gap and warning sections dominate the candidate's narrative.
- A short deadline removes useful answers instead of changing rehearsal priority.
- Company stage, priorities, culture hypotheses, or interview implications are omitted solely because Sprint mode was selected.
- The Battle Card cannot be used without searching the full document for the next item to rehearse.
- A resume is available but the handbook never explains the recruiter's first impression or top validation risks.
- Company or brand identity is ambiguous but the handbook presents brand-specific conclusions as confirmed.
- The document is analytically correct but cannot be practiced aloud as written.
