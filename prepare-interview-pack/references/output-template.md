# Interview Preparation Output Template

Select sections by mode. Preserve this order for included sections.

Maintain a strict layer boundary throughout the artifact:

- **Speakable layer:** introductions, project scripts, question answers, transitions, stop sentences, and culture-fit phrases. Use natural first-person language only. Do not mention source provenance or internal claim statuses.
- **Memory layer:** fact anchors, follow-up prompts, metric definitions, and ownership boundaries. Keep these outside the spoken block.
- **Analysis layer:** source pointers, claim statuses, conflicts, and verification notes. Keep these in the evidence map, verification list, or final claim card.

## 0. Battle Card

Keep this first layer independently usable under time pressure. Target three to five pages in normal Markdown rendering.

- Interview snapshot and immediate schedule
- Recruiter first impression and three validation risks
- One-line role and candidate positioning
- Complete 60-second introduction
- Primary and backup story memory cards: conclusion, three actions, eligible verified or candidate-confirmed result, boundary
- Main gap answer
- Five P0 questions and memory headlines
- Three interviewer questions
- Five-line final fact and prohibition card

Write every complete spoken passage as a Markdown blockquote so the validator can distinguish speech from notes and analysis.

Do not place company research, competency tables, or long warnings between the introduction and story cards.

The Battle Card remains part of the full handbook. A separate 30-minute rapid-review card is a smaller companion artifact; use [rapid-review-card.md](rapid-review-card.md) only when its trigger conditions apply.

## 1. Interview Snapshot

- Company / business unit / role
- Interview deadline and round
- Selected mode and round assumption
- One-sentence hiring-problem diagnosis
- Candidate positioning
- Strong, transferable, and gap summary

## 2. Rehearsal Order

### P0 — Speak Fluently

- 60-second introduction
- Primary story
- Backup story
- Main gap answer
- Three interviewer questions

### P1 — Understand

- JD logic and five competencies
- Round-specific follow-ups
- Required role vocabulary

### P2 — Skim

- Company context
- Secondary examples
- Optional domain detail

Add a time-boxed schedule based on the actual deadline.

## 3. Recruiter First-Impression Diagnostic

- HR and hiring-manager conditional verdict
- One- or two-sentence first impression
- Three decision-relevant risks with classifications
- JD-language and evidence gaps
- Interview questions and proof needed to resolve the risks

Keep this compact; the detailed rubric belongs in the skill reference, not the candidate handbook.

## 4. JD Diagnosis and Competency Ranking

- Hiring problem and outcome groups
- Must-haves, preferred qualifications, hidden constraints
- Five-competency table: rank, competency, score, decisive JD evidence, interview implication

## 5. Company and Role Map

Include this section in every mode. Keep it compact in Sprint mode.

- Identity boundary: recruiting entity, parent company, brand or business unit, and actual team or market
- STARS by confirmed company level, business unit, and role
- Confidence and evidence
- Immediate priorities and culture hypotheses
- Implications for interview answers

## 6. Dream Candidate

- Typical day
- Plain-language superpower
- Representative success pattern
- Synthesized belief statement, explicitly labeled as synthesis
- Pain profile and resolution mechanism

## 7. Requirement-to-Evidence Map

Use columns: JD requirement, match level, project, exact role, eligible result, claim status, gap or risk, answer strategy.

Name the primary, backup, and optional risk story.

### Resume Project Coverage Ledger

When a complete resume is available, list every named project or recent initiative from it. Use columns: resume project, JD relevance, disposition (`P0`, `P1`, `P2`, or `Excluded`), safe speaking scope, result limitation, and handbook location. Evidence uncertainty may narrow the result language but must not silently remove the project.

## 8. Self-Introduction

- One-line positioning
- 60- and 90-second versions; 30-second version when useful
- Delivery notes and approximate timing
- Transition into the primary story

## 9. Project Stories

For each selected story include:

1. Opening conclusion
2. Context and problem
3. Exact candidate role
4. Key judgment or tradeoff
5. Actions
6. Eligible verified or candidate-confirmed results; otherwise state the result limitation without dropping the project
7. Transfer to the target role
8. Three-layer follow-up prompts
9. Factual anchors and overclaim warning
10. Stop sentence and optional expansion directions

## 10. Round-Specific Questions

Include at least ten to twelve questions. Every question gets a complete spoken answer and memory headline. The five P0 questions also get an evidence anchor, likely follow-up, and stop sentence or safe boundary.

## 11. Gap and Culture Answers

- Main gap answer
- Additional material gap answers required by the selected mode
- Culture-fit phrases with the real project each phrase introduces

Keep this section materially shorter than the strengths and project sections.

## 12. Questions for the Interviewer

Provide three questions that reveal priorities, success measures, decision rights, or blockers. Avoid questions answered directly by the JD.

## 13. Minimum Domain Primer

Include only the business chain, actors, metrics, failure modes, and transferable framework needed for credible discussion.

## 14. Rehearsal Scorecard

Score relevance, clarity, credibility, ownership precision, and spoken delivery. Leave candidate-performance scores blank until the candidate actually answers.

## 15. Final Claim Card

Separate:

- Verified and safe to speak
- Candidate-confirmed facts, including eligible user-confirmed metrics with their speaking boundaries
- Derived-safe methods or future approaches
- Confirm before speaking
- Conflicts to resolve
- Inferences, not candidate facts
- Do not use

## 16. Next Action

- Rehearse now
- Review later
- Verify before speaking
