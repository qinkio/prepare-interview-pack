# Routing and Scoring

## Output Mode

### Sprint — Under 24 Hours

Produce the complete core answer library, but prioritize:

- An independently usable Battle Card before the full handbook.
- Interview snapshot and role diagnosis.
- Compact recruiter first impression and three validation risks when a resume is available.
- Compact company-and-role STARS map with stage, priorities, culture hypotheses, and interview implications.
- P0 rehearsal order and time-boxed plan.
- Five-competency ranking.
- One-line positioning plus 60- and 90-second introductions.
- Primary and backup project stories.
- Ten to twelve highest-probability questions with complete spoken answers.
- One main gap answer.
- Three interviewer questions.
- Final claim card and verification list.

Keep supporting analysis compact. Exclude broad company history, secondary research, and nonessential industry study, but retain the compact STARS map. Do not remove core answers because time is short; label only the first five items as P0.

### Standard — 1–3 Days or Unknown

Produce the Sprint content plus:

- STARS and dream-candidate analysis.
- Full requirement-to-evidence map.
- Full recruiter first-impression diagnostic with HR and hiring-manager lenses.
- A 30-second introduction when useful.
- Additional round-specific follow-ups beyond the core ten to twelve questions.
- Culture-fit phrases tied to evidence.
- Compact domain primer.
- Self-rehearsal scorecard.

### Deep — More Than 3 Days

Produce the Standard content plus:

- Current company research with authoritative sources.
- Secondary conflict, failure, or risk story.
- Multiple-round question strategy.
- Expanded domain primer.
- Live mock-interview plan and revision log when the candidate participates.

## Interview-Round Lens

Use the stated round. If unknown, label `Hiring Manager assumption` and include only essential HR risks.

| Round | Allocate attention | De-emphasize |
|---|---|---|
| HR | motivation, transitions, stability, compensation, basic fit | deep implementation detail |
| Hiring Manager | judgment, ownership, metrics, prioritization, execution | generic company praise |
| Cross-functional | alignment, conflict, influence, dependency management | long career chronology |
| Final | strategy, maturity, values, leadership range, long-term fit | feature-level minutiae |
| Mixed | hiring-manager core plus one section for each confirmed attendee | speculative attendee profiling |

## Story Selection Rubric

Score each candidate story out of 100:

- Role-problem directness, 0–30: directly solves the central business problem in the JD.
- Personal ownership, 0–20: shows a clear decision and actions personally owned by the candidate.
- Verified outcome, 0–20: includes a defensible result relevant to the target role.
- Decision depth, 0–15: demonstrates diagnosis, prioritization, tradeoffs, or resource allocation.
- Follow-up defendability, 0–15: supports detailed questions on context, actions, metrics, and limitations.

Require at least 70 for a primary story. Prefer a direct domain story over a more polished adjacent system story. If none scores 70, request a missing role-critical example; under urgent timing, use the highest-scoring story as `provisional` and disclose the missing proof.

## Competency Ranking Rubric

Score each plausible standard competency out of 100, then rank the top five:

- Frequency, 0–30: normalize meaningful mentions relative to other competencies.
- Placement, 0–20: reward title/summary, responsibilities, and required qualifications more than preferred qualifications.
- Explicit ownership, 0–25: reward language such as own, lead, independently, accountable, or drive.
- Business impact, 0–15: reward direct connection to revenue, cost, risk, growth, quality, or delivery.
- Cross-section recurrence, 0–10: reward appearance across multiple JD sections.

Show scores as analytical estimates. Include the decisive words or faithful paraphrases from the JD so the ranking can be audited. When scores are close, explain the tie-break using business impact and required-qualification placement.

## STARS Confidence

Assess parent company, business unit, and role separately.

- High: multiple current authoritative sources align.
- Medium: JD plus at least one authoritative source align.
- Low: mostly inferred from the JD.
- Not classifiable: evidence cannot distinguish stages.

## Spoken-Length Guidance

Time answers aloud when possible. Use these approximate drafting limits:

| Answer | Chinese | English |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30-second introduction | 100–150 Chinese characters | 60–85 words |
| 60-second introduction | 200–280 Chinese characters | 125–165 words |
| 90-second introduction | 300–420 Chinese characters | 185–235 words |
| Project story | about 2–3 spoken minutes | about 2–3 spoken minutes |

Treat these as drafting ranges, not rigid quotas. Prefer actual rehearsal timing.

## Rehearsal Scorecard

Score each response from 1 to 5:

- Relevance: answers the asked question and connects to the role.
- Clarity: has one clear conclusion and an easy-to-follow sequence.
- Credibility: uses verified facts without inflated certainty.
- Ownership precision: distinguishes personal work from team outcomes.
- Spoken delivery: sounds natural and fits the intended duration.

Revise any P0 answer scoring below 4 on credibility or ownership precision before further memorization.

## Selling Balance

Use roughly 70–80% of spoken-answer content to prove strengths, judgment, actions, and results. Limit gap and warning language to roughly 20–30%. Pair each gap with adjacent proof and a credible adaptation plan.
