# Recruiter First-Impression Lens

Use this only to predict interview framing and validation risks. It does not replace resume tailoring.

## Choose the Available View

- Prefer the rendered first page when a PDF is available. Inspect title, summary, recent role, company names, dates, metrics, density, and visual order.
- When only extracted text is available, label the result `content-order simulation`; do not claim to have tested real ten-second visual prominence.

## Simulate Two Screeners

### HR screen

Check hard gates and obvious risk: relevant years, industry or function, level, education when required, location, stability, and unexplained transitions.

### Hiring-manager screen

Check whether the candidate has solved the central role problem, personally owned a comparable decision, produced a defensible outcome, and can operate at the required scope.

## Produce a Compact Diagnostic

1. **Conditional verdict:** `clear fit`, `borderline pending validation`, or `clear mismatch`. State that the result is based only on the supplied JD and resume, not the real applicant pool.
2. **First impression:** one or two sentences describing who the candidate appears to be, the core experience, and the most visible match.
3. **Top three risks:** classify each as `hard gap`, `unclear expression`, `weak evidence`, or `experience ordering` and tie it to exact JD language.
4. **JD-language gaps:** classify important terms as `missing`, `synonymous evidence`, `possibly done but unclear`, `adjacent only`, or `not supported`.
5. **Evidence weakness:** inspect only role-critical claims. Flag vague verbs such as responsible for, participated, followed up, assisted, or optimized when the claim lacks ownership, decision, scale, comparison, or result.
6. **Interview conversion:** turn each material risk into a likely interviewer question, the strongest truthful proof, and the remaining boundary.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent a numeric interview probability or claim knowledge of the actual candidate pool.
- Do not reject merely to sound strict; every negative judgment must change a hiring decision or follow-up question.
- Do not treat keyword absence as experience absence when a faithful synonym exists.
- Do not recommend keyword stuffing.
- If the candidate already has an interview, replace pass/fail language with `why they agreed to meet` and `what they still need to validate`.
